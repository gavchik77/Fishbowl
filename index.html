Understanding the model

How do the alleles interact?

Each fish carries two alleles of one gene, R or r. RR fish are red, Rr fish are pink and rr fish are white. The heterozygote is an intermediate colour (incomplete dominance). Each parent passes one allele to its offspring at random. For instance, Rr × Rr produces expected genotype proportions of ¼ RR, ½ Rr and ¼ rr. A pink fish has two alleles, not a separate “pink allele”.

Genetic drift

Random sampling changes allele frequency across generations, particularly when populations are small. Even without selection, migration or mutation, one allele may eventually be lost or fixed.

Natural selection

Use the relative survival sliders to give the genotypes different probabilities of contributing to the next generation. A genotype with greater survival tends to contribute more genes, though drift may obscure the trend in small populations.

Migration (gene flow)

Migrants enter from an external source population whose R frequency is independently adjustable. Their alleles may change the aquarium population and can restore alleles previously lost.

Mutation

R can change to r and r can change to R during offspring formation. Realistic mutation rates are often much lower than the large teaching values offered here; the exaggerated settings make the mechanism easier to observe.

Non-random mating and Hardy–Weinberg

Positive assortative mating makes genetically similar fish more likely to pair; negative assortative mating favours unlike genotypes. Assortative mating can change genotype proportions even when allele frequencies do not systematically change. Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium predicts RR = p², Rr = 2pq and rr = q² after random mating under ideal assumptions. Random sampling in finite populations still introduces drift.

How this educational model differs from the original

This is independently written simulation software, not a copy of Virtual Biology Lab code. Fish are animated onscreen but breeding occurs in discrete, non-overlapping generations with a configurable density-dependent population target, not through encounters in a spatial simulation. Selection is applied to potential parents, followed by mate choice and Mendelian inheritance. Migration replaces a fraction of the new generation. This makes the main evolutionary processes observable but does not reproduce the original model’s exact individual lifecycle, overlapping generations, Allee effects or parameter meanings.
